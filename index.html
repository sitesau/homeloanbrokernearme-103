Home Loan Brokers Brisbane Documents Needed Explained

Browsing mortgage brokers in Brisbane often involves categorising the enquiry by purpose. Whether the goal is a first purchase, a refinance, an investment loan or construction finance, the documents required differ. Brokers use these documents to compare how different lenders weigh factors such as income, expenses and credit history. Preparing the right paperwork before the first conversation ensures the broker can focus on finding suitable options rather than chasing missing information. A short brief covering the property goal, rough timing and finance type keeps the first meeting focused. Borrowers should prepare documents on income, existing debts, savings and identification before meeting a broker to streamline the assessment process.

First home buyer documents

First home buyers should prepare proof of identity such as a passport or driver's licence, recent payslips or income evidence, bank statements covering everyday accounts and evidence of savings or deposit funds. If the borrower intends to claim government grants or concessions, evidence of eligibility for these schemes should also be gathered. This preparation helps the broker determine if the borrower qualifies for specific first-home buyer programs offered by lenders. Concessions and grants often have strict eligibility criteria, so having the correct paperwork ready avoids delays in the approval process.

Refinancing documents

Refinancing requires a clear picture of the current loan position. Borrowers need the current loan statement, the remaining balance and details of existing loans and credit cards. It is also helpful to have a repayment history to show how the borrower has managed the debt. Understanding the costs of switching, such as exit fees, is part of the assessment process. Brokers can explain how existing-loan information is compared against alternatives and whether switching costs affect the outcome. Having these documents ready allows the borrower to make an informed decision about whether refinancing is the right financial move.

Investment loan documents

Investment loans involve assessing rental income and tax implications. Borrowers should provide evidence of rental income, such as lease agreements or bank statements, and tax returns. Details of existing debts and credit cards are also necessary to calculate the loan-to-value ratio. Brokers can explain how negative gearing and interest-only structures might affect the loan terms. Rental income expectations and repayment type are assessed differently for investment loans compared to owner-occupied loans. Providing accurate tax and income documentation ensures the broker can find a loan that aligns with the borrower's investment strategy.

Construction documents

Construction finance requires more detailed plans. Lenders typically need building contracts, architectural plans and details of the builder. Staged funding requirements and progress payments are also assessed. Having these documents ready allows the broker to find lenders who specialise in construction loans. Details of the builder, including their licence number, are often required for specialist lending. Building contracts and plans are usually required before a lender can consider the request. This level of detail helps the broker identify lenders with experience in construction projects and assess the risk associated with the build.

  1. Define loan purpose. Decide whether the enquiry is a first purchase, refinance, investment or construction loan because each is assessed differently.
  2. Gather documents. Collect identification, income evidence, bank statements, existing loan details and deposit or savings records.
  3. Write a brief. Summarise the property goal, rough timing, current loan position and finance type so the broker has full context.
  4. Contact broker. Share the brief and documents to start the comparison process and confirm fees, lender access and availability.

Common questions

Do I need to bring originals? Usually copies are sufficient for the initial comparison, but originals may be requested later for verification.

What if I am self-employed? You will need business financials and tax returns to prove income and demonstrate repayment capacity.

Can I email documents? Yes, most brokers accept digital copies for the initial assessment to save time before the first meeting.
